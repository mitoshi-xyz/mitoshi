Mitoshi's promise is narrow and checkable: **nobody can link two of your trading wallets, or any of them to you or your
ZEC.** It hides who traded, not what was traded.

## Who sees what

<OhttpHops />

| Party | Sees | Never sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your browser** | Everything: your words, balance and which wallets are yours, encrypted on this device | — it sends none of it in the clear |
| **Relay** (Cloudflare, `mitoshi-web`) | Your IP, the size and time of each request, and which public markets you look at | What you ask about your wallets: every such call is sealed |
| **Gateway** (Vercel, Dublin) | One request's content, such as an address's balance, arriving from the relay | Your IP, cookies, or which requests come from the same person |
| **Each venue** | That one wallet's orders and positions, sent from Ireland | Your other wallets, your balance, your IP or country |
| **NEAR Intents** | One swap: a standard ZEC amount in and a fresh wallet out (a funding), or a venue asset in and a fresh shielded address out (a trip home) | Who sent it, or your other swaps |
| **The Zcash chain** | That a shielded transaction happened, and the one hop to NEAR's deposit address | Amounts and addresses inside the pool, or that it was you |
| **Mitoshi's servers** | Only what the relay and gateway rows say; there is no database and no account | Your keys, your wallet list, your positions |

## What keeps your wallets apart

- **One wallet per position.** An order goes to the wallet that already holds that market; otherwise to a ready wallet
  that has never traded; otherwise you fund a new one. Another position's wallet is never used
  (`positionWallet`, `packages/engine/src/registry.ts`).
- **Its own key** on a hardened path of Mitoshi's own, so no two wallets share a key, a refund address or a request.
- **Standard sizes at random moments.** See [the rules](/docs/privacy/rules).
- **Every call sealed.** See [Oblivious HTTP](/docs/privacy/oblivious-http).
