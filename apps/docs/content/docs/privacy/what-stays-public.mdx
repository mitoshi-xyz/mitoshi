## Public on purpose

| What | Why it is public |
| --- | --- |
| **Each wallet's own trades** on its venue | The venues are public markets; Mitoshi hides who traded, not what |
| **One hop in and one hop out** per wallet, through NEAR | The funding pays NEAR's deposit address at a standard size many swaps share. The trip home carries whatever the wallet holds, so its amount is public; it lands at a fresh shielded address, and the leak meter warns if you later withdraw a matching amount publicly |
| **NEAR's record of each swap** | Confidential mode, where a deposit and its payout can't be matched, covers every funding and every trip home except Polymarket's. That one pays out through Polymarket's bridge with a flexible-input quote, and 1Click has no confidential mode for those |
| **Polymarket's builder attribution** on each wallet's gasless relayer calls (creating it, approvals, redeeming, moving pUSD into Perps, the trip home) | Polymarket can tell these come from Mitoshi users, and when, not which one |
| **Which public markets an IP looks at** | Prices, books and list pictures come through the relay unsealed. Your wallets, orders, balances and the pictures of what you hold go sealed |

<Callout title="What 'confidential' means in the app">
Mitoshi asks for confidential mode on every quote it can; that needs NEAR's partner key on the gateway. If 1Click
refuses a confidential quote (HTTP 401), the app uses public quotes for the rest of the session. The funding sheet's
route line and the Privacy screen show the mode NEAR **granted**, read back from the signed quote: "confidential" or
"public", in amber when public.
</Callout>

## What Mitoshi cannot hide

<Callout type="warn" title="Read these before you trust it">

- **One operator** runs the relay and the gateway and serves the app. Keeping the two apart is a promise you can check
  in the source, not something the code enforces.
- **Reading your wallets.** While Portfolio or a terminal is open, the gateway and the venues see those balances asked
  about in the same stretch of time. Each wallet is read on its own random rhythm (every 4–8 s on a terminal, every
  20–60 s on Portfolio), through one gateway address
  shared by everyone, but a watcher of many sessions could still notice two addresses always asked about together.
- **Timing analysis.** Random starts and rests make it hard, not impossible, for someone with the whole public record.
- **The recovery scan** asks about one derived address at a time, 3–9 s apart. Someone watching the gateway during a
  restore could still group them, so it runs only after a restore from words or when you ask for it.
- **Moving Orchard to Ironwood.** Each part of a migration publishes one standard amount crossing pools at a random
  time, the same shape as every other ZIP 318 migration; payments never cross.
- **Block explorers.** Opening one for your wallet from your own browser shows it your IP. Mitoshi's "What the public
  sees" reads the same data through the gateway; use Tor or a VPN for explorers.
- **Light servers.** Compact blocks come from ChainSafe's public servers, which only ever see the gateway, until Mitoshi
  runs its own.
- **The app must be open.** Fundings, keep-ready and swap tracking run in your tab; nothing runs on a server for you.

</Callout>
