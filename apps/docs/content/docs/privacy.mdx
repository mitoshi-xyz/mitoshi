The promise is narrow and checkable: **nobody can link two of your trading wallets, or any of them to you or your
ZEC.** Mitoshi hides who traded, not what was traded.

## Who sees what

<OhttpHops />

| Party | Sees | Does not see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your browser** | Everything: words, balance, which wallets are yours | — it sends none of it in the clear |
| **Relay** (Cloudflare, `mitoshi-web`) | Your IP, the size and time of each request, which public markets you look at | What you ask about your wallets: every such call is sealed |
| **Gateway** (Vercel, Dublin) | One request's content, such as an address's balance, from a Cloudflare address | Your IP, cookies, or which requests come from the same person |
| **Each venue** | That one wallet's orders and positions, from Ireland | Your other wallets, your balance, your IP |
| **NEAR Intents** | One swap: a standard ZEC amount in, a fresh wallet out, a fresh refund address | Who sent it, or your other swaps |
| **Zcash chain** | That a shielded transaction happened, and the one hop to NEAR's deposit address | Amounts and addresses inside the pool, or that it was you |
| **Mitoshi's servers** | Only what the relay and gateway rows say. There is no database and no account | Your keys, your wallet list, your positions |

## Rules that keep wallets apart

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| One new key per wallet, on a hardened path | No two wallets share an address |
| Fundings only at standard sizes, 0.01 to 1 ZEC | The one public hop blends in with others |
| A random start, 20 s to 3 min after you ask | The hop is not timed to your tap |
| Keep-ready refills wait 90 min on average, 12 h at most | A new wallet never follows your trade |
| Value from a public address rests 2–6 h | Its arrival is not matched to your next trade |
| A fresh shielded refund address per swap, a fresh payout address per trip home | Swaps share no address |
| No memos, the standard transaction shape | Nothing marks the payment |
| No builder code on orders | Orders don't name Mitoshi on the venue |
| After a send, no sync for a random 1–10 min | A broadcast and the same wallet's block reads never share a window |
| Each wallet's reads on its own random schedule | The gateway sees no batch of your addresses at once |

## The leak meter

Every send runs through the leak meter (`packages/engine/src/leak.ts`).

| Send | Red | Amber |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funding a wallet | Not a standard size, or it would spend resting money: **blocked** | — |
| Withdrawal to a public address | Exactly matches money that came home in the last 48 h | Within 2% of it, or within 2 h of a payout |
| Withdrawal to a shielded address | — | — (always clear) |

On withdrawals red warns rather than blocks: sending to your own exchange is your choice.

## Oblivious HTTP

Each call is encrypted to the gateway's key (RFC 9458: X25519, AES-128-GCM, a fresh key per request) and posted to
the relay. The gateway's key is **pinned in the app**, so the relay cannot swap in its own. The gateway calls only an
allowlist of hosts and paths, drops cookies, origin, referrer, user agent and forwarding headers, and sends one shared
user agent for everyone.

The wallet is no exception: even block ranges are sealed, because where a scan starts reveals a wallet's birthday.

## What stays public

- **Each wallet's own trades** on its venue.
- **One hop in and one hop out** per wallet, at a standard size, through NEAR's deposit addresses.
- **NEAR's record of each swap.** Confidential mode, in which a deposit and its payout can't be matched, is **on**
  (the gateway holds NEAR's partner key) for every funding and every trip home **except Polymarket's**, which pays out
  through a flexible-input quote that has no confidential mode.
- **Polymarket's builder attribution** on each wallet's gasless relayer calls: Polymarket knows these come from Mitoshi
  users, and when, not which one.
- **Which public markets an IP looks at.** Prices, books and list pictures come through the relay unsealed. Your
  wallets, orders, balances and the pictures of what you hold go sealed.

## What Mitoshi cannot hide

<Callout type="warn" title="Read these before you trust it">

- **One operator** runs the relay and the gateway and serves the app. Keeping the two apart is a promise you can check
  in the source, not something the code enforces.
- **Reading your wallets.** While Portfolio or a terminal is open, the gateway and the venues see those balances asked
  about in the same stretch of time. Each wallet is read on its own random schedule, through one gateway address shared
  by everyone, but a watcher of many sessions could still notice two addresses always asked about together. Keep fewer
  wallets open at once if that matters to you.
- **Timing analysis.** Random starts and rests make it hard, not impossible, for someone with the whole public record.
- **The recovery scan** asks about one derived address at a time, 3–9 s apart. Someone watching the gateway during a
  restore could still group them, so it runs only when you restore from words.
- **Block explorers.** Opening one for your wallet from your own browser shows it your IP. Mitoshi's "What the public
  sees" view reads the same data through the relay; use Tor or a VPN for explorers.
- **Light servers.** Compact blocks come from ChainSafe's public servers, which only ever see the gateway, until
  Mitoshi runs its own.

</Callout>
